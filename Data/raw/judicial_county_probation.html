About Colorado Probation

The Colorado Judicial Department administers adult and juvenile probation within Colorado’s 23 judicial districts. This includes 24 probation departments with over 70 separate probation offices throughout the state. The Colorado Probation Statement of Common Ground, listed above, was developed to clearly identify the critical functions that unify all probation departments in carrying out their mission. Probation’s commitment to these practices requires the implementation of innovative approaches to offender assessments, supervision, victim involvement and services to the community. These probation departments strive to meet every part of the Statement of Common Ground on a daily basis.

Follow Us:

Judge William Young Furse is a District Court Judge for the 22nd Judicial District of Colorado and presides over criminal, civil, probate, domestic relations, juvenile matters, and appeals.  

Judge Furse received his bachelor’s degree from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign in 2001 and earned his law degree in 2005 from John Marshall Law School in Chicago, Illinois. 

Prior to taking the bench, Judge Furse began his legal career in Durango, Colorado, serving as a Deputy State Public Defender for the 22nd and 6th Judicial Districts.

From 2009 to 2012, Judge Furse worked in private practice as a solo practitioner focused on criminal defense and served as a contract public defender for the Ute Mountain Ute Tribe.

From 2012 to 2020, Judge Furse served two terms as the elected District Attorney for the 22nd Judicial District.  Following his term-limited service, Judge Furse was Assistant District Attorney for the 22nd District and briefly returned to private practice in 2022 until his appointment to the bench on January 10th, 2023.
